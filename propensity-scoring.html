Case Study 02

Helping Sales Teams Prioritize the Right Accounts for Co-sell

Role Product Manager
Company Labra
Partnered with
Engineering Sales Customer Success RevOps QA GTM Design

01

The Challenge

One question came up repeatedly from customers:

“We have hundreds of accounts and opportunities. Which ones should we actually take to AWS or Microsoft?”

Submitting everything wasn’t practical. Partner teams had limited capacity, and sharing low-fit opportunities created additional work without necessarily improving co-sell outcomes.

Customers already had plenty of data — firmographics, technology usage, cloud signals, engagement history, and CRM information.

The real problem was turning those fragmented signals into a decision.

The product question became: how could we help Sales and Partner teams identify which accounts deserved their attention — and give them enough confidence to act on the recommendation?

02

What We Learned

Discovery showed us that a “good account” wasn’t universal.

Each customer had a different product, market, GTM motion, and definition of their ideal buyer. And an account that strongly matched a customer’s ICP wasn’t necessarily a strong candidate for a marketplace or co-sell motion.

Our initial approach combined multiple signals into an overall propensity score. As we tested and iterated with customers, we learned that a single score could create false precision.

Users needed to understand two things separately:

  • Is this account a strong fit for my business?
  • Are there signals that make it promising for a marketplace or co-sell motion?

That distinction became central to how the product evolved.

03

My Role

I led product strategy and execution for Propensity to Buy (PTB), from customer discovery and problem definition through requirements, rollout, and iteration.

I worked closely with Engineering to translate customer prioritization needs into a multi-signal scoring framework — defining which signals should contribute to account prioritization, how they should be interpreted, and how the resulting recommendations should be presented to customers.

I also owned the ICP framework, evaluated internal and third-party data sources, and drove improvements to the scoring approach based on customer feedback and data quality.

04

Key Product Decisions

Separate account fit from marketplace propensity

Rather than relying solely on one overall score, we evolved the product around two clearer dimensions:

  • ICP Fit — how closely does this account match the customer’s ideal buyer?
  • Marketplace Propensity — what signals suggest this account could be a strong candidate for a cloud marketplace or co-sell motion?

Separating the two gave users more context behind a recommendation instead of hiding different signals inside a single score.

Make prioritization customer-specific

Every customer defined an ideal account differently, so a universal ICP would have produced generic recommendations.

We built a configurable ICP framework that allowed customers to define the firmographic, technographic, and other characteristics that mattered to their GTM strategy.

The question shifted from “Which companies are good?” to “Which companies are good for your business?”

Prioritize trust over a black-box score

As the product evolved, we learned that generating a recommendation wasn’t enough. Customers needed to understand why an account was prioritized and how much confidence they should place in the underlying signals.

We made recommendations more explainable by exposing contributing factors and addressed an important data-quality problem: missing data doesn’t necessarily mean low propensity.

Instead of treating missing signals as negative signals, we began distinguishing between low fit and insufficient evidence, while evaluating additional data sources to improve coverage.

This made explainability and data quality part of the product experience rather than simply implementation concerns.

05

What We Shipped

  • Account-level Propensity to Buy recommendations
  • Configurable Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) framework
  • Separate views of account fit and marketplace propensity
  • Multi-signal evaluation using firmographic, technographic, engagement, and ecosystem signals
  • Explainable recommendations showing contributing factors
  • Handling for insufficient data and continuous improvements to scoring and data coverage

Outcome

~40%

increase in qualified opportunities for an early customer

During early adoption, PTB and ICP-based prioritization helped customers focus their Sales and Partner teams on accounts with stronger fit and co-sell potential.

Instead of treating every opportunity equally, teams could use multiple signals to prioritize higher-quality accounts, improve qualification, and make co-sell decisions with better evidence.

The product shifted the conversation from “Which opportunities do we have?” to “Which opportunities deserve our attention?”

What I Learned

Customers don’t need more data — they need confidence in their decisions.

My biggest learning from PTB was that building an intelligent product isn’t about creating the most sophisticated-looking score.

Users consistently came back to three questions:

  • Why is this account recommended?
  • Can I trust the underlying data?
  • What should I do with this recommendation?

That changed how I approached the product. Explainability, data quality, and communicating uncertainty became just as important as the scoring methodology itself.

The goal wasn’t to replace sales judgment — it was to give teams better evidence to make that judgment.
