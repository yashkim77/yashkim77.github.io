Case Study 02

Helping Sales Teams Prioritize the Right Account for Co-sell Opportunities

Role Product Manager
Company Labra
Partnered with
Engineering Data Science Customer Success Sales GTM

01

The Challenge

One question came up repeatedly from customers: "We have hundreds of opportunities. Which ones should we actually share with AWS or Microsoft?"

Submitting every opportunity to a hyperscaler wasn't practical. It created unnecessary work for partner teams and often resulted in low-quality referrals that had little chance of success.

Customers needed a way to identify which accounts were the strongest candidates for co-selling before investing time in the process.

The challenge was that this decision depended on many factors — not just company size or industry, but cloud adoption, firmographic data, technographic signals, engagement patterns, and how closely an account matched the customer's ideal customer profile (ICP).

02

My Role

I led the product strategy and execution for Propensity to Buy (PTB), a product designed to help customers prioritize the right accounts for co-selling. My responsibilities included:

  • Leading customer discovery to understand how sales teams prioritized accounts
  • Defining the scoring methodology and product requirements
  • Designing the account scoring algorithm with Engineering
  • Evaluating third-party data sources to improve prediction quality
  • Defining the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) framework
  • Driving implementation, testing, rollout, and iterative improvements based on customer feedback

Rather than relying on a single signal, I worked on creating a scoring framework that combined multiple data sources into one actionable recommendation.

03

Key Product Decision #1 — Make the scoring explainable, not just predictive

Customers didn't just want a score — they wanted to understand why an account was flagged as high priority. We made the model explainable by showing the key factors behind each recommendation, such as firmographic fit, technographic signals, engagement history, and other contributing signals.

That transparency mattered because it helped customers trust the output instead of treating it as a black box. When they could see why the system was recommending an account, they were far more likely to use it in real sales decisions.

The goal wasn't to replace sales judgment — it was to build trust in the prioritization process and make it easier for teams to act on it.

04

Key Product Decision #2 — Make account prioritization configurable through ICPs

Every company defines its ideal customer differently. Instead of shipping one universal definition, we built a configurable Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) framework.

Customers could define the characteristics of their target accounts, allowing the platform to evaluate how closely every account matched their business. This meant two companies using the same platform could receive completely different recommendations based on their own go-to-market strategy.

The combination of PTB and configurable ICPs helped customers prioritize both:

  • Which accounts fit their business
  • Which accounts were most likely to succeed in a co-sell motion

05

What We Shipped

  • Propensity to Buy scoring for every account
  • Configurable Ideal Customer Profile framework
  • Multi-factor account evaluation using internal and third-party data
  • Prioritized account recommendations
  • Explainable scoring that helped sales teams understand recommendations
  • Continuous model improvements based on customer feedback and new data

Outcome

Although the product was still evolving, we saw meaningful early impact with the first set of customers. In the first few months, customers using the product were able to prioritize higher-quality co-sell opportunities more effectively, leading to roughly a 40% increase in qualified pipeline opportunities.

The product helped customers:

  • Prioritize higher-quality co-sell opportunities
  • Increase qualified pipeline opportunities by roughly 40% in the early rollout phase
  • Improve account qualification by focusing on opportunities with stronger fit and better conversion potential
  • Make co-sell decisions using data rather than intuition

Reflection

Customers don't need more data — they need confidence in their decisions.
