Case Study 01

Building a Multi-Hyperscaler Co-Sell Automation Platform

Role Product Manager
Company Labra
Partnered with
Engineering Sales Customer Success RevOps QA GTM Design

01

The Challenge

Enterprise companies running co-sell programs managed the same opportunity across two systems — their CRM (Salesforce or HubSpot) and hyperscaler partner portals such as AWS Partner Central or Microsoft Partner Center.

Sales teams primarily worked in the CRM, while Partner teams worked in hyperscaler portals. Every deal update often had to be manually reflected across both.

As co-sell programs scaled, this created duplicate work, inconsistent data, and slower deal execution.

The product question was: how could we let teams run co-sell from the systems they already use while keeping CRM and hyperscaler workflows reliably synchronized?

02

What We Learned

Customer discovery showed us that this wasn’t simply a data-sync problem.

Every customer had different CRM fields, workflows, validation rules, and ownership models. At the same time, each hyperscaler had its own APIs and opportunity lifecycle.

We also learned an important lesson from our first Salesforce release.

In V1, we leaned heavily into a native Salesforce experience because we wanted Sales teams to manage co-sell where they already worked. While this improved adoption, it introduced complexity as we scaled — particularly around package upgrades, customer-specific Salesforce environments, and our ability to evolve the product quickly.

For V2, we moved toward a hybrid approach: keeping experiences native where they improved the user’s workflow, while moving capabilities that needed greater flexibility outside the constraints of the Salesforce package.

Be native where it creates user value; stay independent where it creates product leverage.

That became an important principle as we evolved the platform.

03

My Role

I owned the product end-to-end, from customer discovery and roadmap definition through requirements, delivery, rollout, and iteration.

I worked closely with Engineering, Sales, Customer Success, RevOps, and Design to translate complex customer and hyperscaler workflows into a scalable product.

My engineering background helped me work deeply with Engineering on API constraints, synchronization patterns, and architecture trade-offs, while my focus as PM remained on what problems we should solve, how the experience should work, and what would scale across customers and ecosystems.

04

Key Product Decisions

Build a platform, not a collection of integrations

The quickest approach was to build separate point-to-point integrations for Salesforce ↔ AWS, HubSpot ↔ AWS, and eventually Microsoft.

Instead, we designed Labra as an orchestration layer that could apply mappings and business rules while synchronizing opportunities across connected systems.

This required more upfront investment but created a reusable foundation that later allowed us to expand from AWS to Microsoft Azure without rebuilding the product from scratch.

Make workflows configurable

Enterprise CRM implementations varied significantly across customers.

Rather than hard-coding Labra around a standard CRM schema, we made mappings and workflow behavior configurable so customers could adapt Labra to their existing processes instead of changing their CRM to fit our product.

Balance native experience with scalability

V1 taught us that maximizing native functionality wasn’t necessarily the same as creating the best long-term product.

For V2, we kept CRM-native experiences where they reduced context switching, while moving capabilities outside the native package when doing so improved upgradeability, maintainability, and iteration speed.

The goal wasn’t to make everything native. It was to find the right boundary between user experience and product scalability.

05

What We Shipped

  • Synchronization across Salesforce, HubSpot, AWS Partner Central, and Microsoft Partner Center
  • Salesforce and HubSpot experiences for managing co-sell workflows from the CRM
  • Automated opportunity creation, field and stage synchronization
  • Configurable field mappings and workflow rules
  • Notifications, validations, synchronization status, and error handling
  • A reusable platform foundation that expanded from AWS to Microsoft Azure

Outcome

~65%

average reduction in customer deal lifecycle time

2

hyperscalers supported through the same platform foundation

The platform reduced repetitive manual updates between CRM and hyperscaler portals and allowed Sales and Partner teams to operate with more consistent opportunity data.

Customers could manage co-sell closer to their existing CRM workflows, while the underlying platform handled synchronization across systems.

The architecture also scaled from AWS to Microsoft Azure without requiring a complete redesign, and some enterprise customers reported up to ~80% reduction in manual operational effort.

What I Learned

Automation only works when users trust it.

As customers stopped manually checking every update, we learned that synchronization itself wasn’t enough.

Users needed confidence that opportunities had synced correctly — and clear visibility when they hadn’t.

That made validation, error handling, reconciliation, and status visibility part of the product experience rather than purely technical concerns.

The broader lesson for me was that building scalable integration products requires balancing three things: where users want to work, how reliably the system behaves, and how easily the product can evolve.
